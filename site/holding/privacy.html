Privacy

This page says what Idiograph and this website keep about you, why, and for how long.

I am Adam Knowles. I make Idiograph, and I am responsible for the data on this page. In the law's words, I am the data controller. Write to me at hello@idiograph.app about anything here.

This website

The website sets no cookies and keeps nothing in your browser. It has no analytics and counts no one. Its fonts are your computer's own, and its scripts and pictures come from idiograph.app itself.

Cloudflare serves the website. Like any web server, it sees your internet address in order to send you the pages.

The Download page works out what kind of computer you have, so that it can offer the right download. It does this in your browser, and the answer is never sent anywhere. The download itself comes from GitHub, which sees your internet address.

Updates by email

You can ask for an email when there is a new version or news of Idiograph. You sign up on this website or in the app's Settings. I send a few emails a year.

  • I keep your email address and whether you have confirmed it. I also keep where you signed up (the website or the app), the date and time, which wording of the sign-up you agreed to, and the app's version if you signed up in the app.
  • Until you confirm, I also keep your internet address in a scrambled form (a salted hash), so that the form cannot be used to send many emails. It is deleted when you confirm.
  • To stop the form being misused, I keep a short record for each sign-up. It holds the time, and your email and internet addresses in a scrambled form. A record can last up to three days before it is deleted.

I use your address only to send you these emails. The lawful basis is your consent, and you can withdraw it at any time.

Your address stays on the list until you leave. Every email has a link to leave with one click, and leaving deletes your address. If you do not confirm a sign-up, it is deleted 7 days after the latest confirmation email.

If an email to you cannot be delivered, or you mark one as spam, Amazon stops sending to your address, and I delete it from the list before the next email goes out.

Cloudflare stores the list in its Western Europe region. Amazon sends the emails from its London service, Amazon SES.

Send feedback

The bug button in the app sends me a note when you press Send. The note carries:

  • What you write, and a reply address if you give one.
  • The app's version and your computer's system, such as macOS 15.5.
  • The name of the screen you were on, such as PETs.
  • The user agent of the app's window, which names its browser engine and system.

The service that receives it adds the time, and your internet address in a scrambled form (a salted hash). The hash lets it accept at most 30 notes an hour from one sender. Nothing of your projects or participants is sent. No picture of the screen is taken.

I use the notes to fix what you report and to answer you if you ask. The lawful basis is my legitimate interest in improving Idiograph and in answering the people who use it. You choose to send each note.

Notes are kept until I delete them. Nothing deletes them on a timer. Write to me and I will delete yours.

Cloudflare stores the notes.

Working together

Working together passes your project's changes between your team's computers through a relay at idiograph.app. Everything is sealed on your computer before it leaves, and the relay cannot read it. It never sees names, pseudonyms, words or file names.

The relay keeps the sealed messages and files, each computer's public key, a random id for the project, and the times, sizes and number of the messages. It sees each computer's internet address when it connects. It keeps an address only for the day that address started a project, so that one address cannot start too many, and deletes it after that day.

A message or file is deleted once every computer on the project has read it. Anything older than 90 days is deleted, read or not.

The relay keeps this only to pass your updates on, once you turn Working together on. The lawful basis is my legitimate interest in running the service you asked for.

Cloudflare runs the relay, and keeps what it holds in the European Union. If your team shares a folder instead, nothing reaches the relay.

The app on your computer

Idiograph keeps your projects on your own computer, and I never see them. While update checking is on, the app asks GitHub whether a newer version is out, and GitHub sees your internet address and your version. With the AI features on, it sends what you ask to the AI provider you chose. Those services' own privacy policies apply there. The manual lists everything the app sends.

Who I share it with

I never sell anything about you. I share it only with Cloudflare and Amazon, which store and send it for me.

Your rights

You can ask me for a copy of what I keep about you, and ask me to correct it or delete it. You can withdraw your consent to the emails at any time. Write to hello@idiograph.app, and I will answer within a month.

If you are unhappy with how I handle your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK's regulator, at ico.org.uk.
