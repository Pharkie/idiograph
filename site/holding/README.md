`features.html` (Adam, 11 November 2026, the drawn layout C) says what Idiograph does, and what it does not do yet, for a researcher deciding whether it does what they need. The heading stands alone, with no line under it (Adam, 11 November 2026). Each group is a checklist: a feature's name, a tick, Experimental, Planned or Not planned, and its one line, the row opening to its detail. Planned and Not planned (Adam, 12 November 2026, in place of Not yet) follow the harness backlog and his rulings, its won't-do list among them, and inside a group the Planned rows come before the Not planned. A feature done only through an AI agent sits under AI features and agents with a tick, its line saying so (the Through an agent mark went on 12 November 2026). The find matches a row's mark too: "planned" finds the Planned rows, "not planned" the Not planned, "experimental" the Experimental. Key features come first (Adam, 11 November 2026), six cards in two rows of three, each a line icon drawn in the page in the tick's and the warning sign's stroke, the feature's name and its row's one line, each opening its row. Then a find box filters the rows as you type, and a row kept for its detail alone shows the detail's sentence that matched. Below the list comes the download. The Preview's warning is on the home page alone. The words are from an inventory of the app checked against its code on 11 November 2026: when a feature lands or changes, change its row here by hand. `features.js` runs the find and opens a row a link names. Without scripts the find box is hidden and every row is shown and opens by itself.

`get.js` names the visitor's computer from the browser and offers its file from the latest release on GitHub: "Download for Mac" (`Idiograph.dmg`, Apple Silicon), "Download for Windows" (`Idiograph-Setup.exe`), "Download for Linux" (`Idiograph-amd64.deb`, with "Other Linux: AppImage" beside it). An Intel Mac, a phone or tablet, or a computer it cannot tell gets a plain line instead, a phone or tablet "Idiograph is a desktop app for Mac, Windows and Linux. Visit this page on one of those computers to download it." (Adam, 12 November 2026). Below it are always Other downloads (the latest release's page) and Source on GitHub. Without scripts the plain line shows. On the home page, under the download, "Opening it the first time on a Mac" and "Opening it the first time on Windows" each open to the steps the README gives for an app that is not signed yet, the visitor's own computer's open and first and the other closed below it, both closed for any other computer or without scripts. Linux has none, since nothing there stops an app for not being signed.

`about.html` (12 November 2026) is a picture of Adam with a short bio, both from his own site, and his message on why he made Idiograph, then the download. `screenshots.html` (Adam, 12 November 2026, by voice: "little clips showing a piece of action from the app with real screenshots", no play to press, no music) is the Features page's six Key features, each as a short clip of the real app looping in a column, one under another: Transcripts in the shapes you have, IPA's own steps, Longitudinal IPA, Tables for your supervisor and thesis, PETs on a canvas, and Your Excel workbook, on your computer. Each has the feature's name, its Key feature's line word for word, and the clip at three quarters of the page's width (the whole width at 900 pixels or less), playing while it is on the screen and paused when it is not, muted, looping, with no controls and no words laid over it. The clips are in `clips/`, each `<name>.mp4` (H.264), `<name>.webm` (VP9) where that is smaller and listed first, and `<name>.jpg`, its poster, a still near its end. Each is 1920 by 1200, the app's window at 1280 by 800 recorded at twice its size and scaled, 30 frames a second, 17 to 25 seconds, ending on its last frame held a moment and faded into its first. `clips.js` plays them as they come into view. With the visitor's setting for less motion, the posters stay still with a round play button, and a click on one plays it and a second pauses it. Without scripts every clip plays by itself (`autoplay`). The clips are in git, as the film is not (`.gitignore` lets `site/holding/clips/*.mp4` in). They are made again with `packaging/motion/clip.mjs`, a script a clip in `packaging/motion/clips/`, and `packaging/motion/clip-encode.mjs` (`docs/internals.md`, Motion recordings, Clips for the website): the demo for five of them, and for Longitudinal IPA an invented study of three timepoints, made through the command line by `packaging/motion/clips/make_lipa.py`. The workbook's second half is the workbook's own first sheet drawn from the file by `packaging/motion/clips/sheet.py`, never a picture of a spreadsheet program. When a screen a clip shows changes, record that clip again.

`mark.js` is Home's mark code from `family/static/shell.js` (from "the mark coming in on Idiograph's Home" to the end of `homeMarkScene`, at 4278b1d) with its comments left out, as no served file carries a comment. Copy it again from there when Home's ways change. The pages keep nothing on the visitor's machine, set no cookie, count no one, and ask nothing of any other site: their fonts are the computer's own and their scripts and pictures are served from this folder or written in the page. Light or dark follows the visitor's setting. They work from a phone's 360 pixels to a wide screen, and with the keyboard alone. Search engines are asked not to list any page (`noindex`).
