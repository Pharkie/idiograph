Code signing policy

Free code signing provided by SignPath.io, certificate by SignPath Foundation.

What is signed

The Windows installer of each Idiograph app (Idiograph, Sift and Trace) and the programs inside it. Only builds made by the project's own GitHub workflow, from a tagged release of the public repository, are sent to be signed. The source is open under the GNU Affero General Public Licence, version 3.

Signing is switched on once the SignPath Foundation has approved the project. Until then the installer is not signed, and the Download page says how to open it.

Who decides

  • Authors, who write the code: Adam Knowles (Pharkie on GitHub), and anyone whose change is merged into the repository.
  • Reviewers, who review changes to the code: Adam Knowles.
  • Approvers, who approve each signing request in SignPath: Adam Knowles.

Every signing request is approved by hand, one release at a time. Nothing is signed automatically. Each person with a role uses multi-factor authentication on GitHub and on SignPath.

Nothing is signed that was not built from the repository's source by its workflow, and no program is signed that is not part of Idiograph.

Privacy

Idiograph keeps your projects on your own computer. It sends information to another computer only in the cases below.

  • While update checking is on, a few seconds after each start and once a day while it runs, it asks GitHub whether a newer version is out. GitHub sees your internet address and the version you have, and nothing of your work.
  • Send feedback sends the note you write, a reply address if you give one, the app's version, your system and the name of the screen you were on, when you press Send.
  • With the AI features switched on, an AI control sends one passage, or one PET or GET, to the provider you chose, when you press it. They are off on a new install.
  • An AI agent you use yourself with Idiograph sends whatever it reads to its own provider, which can be a whole participant.
  • Working together sends sealed updates to your teammates through the relay at idiograph.app, or through a folder you share. The relay cannot read them. It sees each computer's internet address and the times, sizes and number of the messages.
  • When you press Download on a demo's player, it fetches the demo's recordings from GitHub.
  • In Settings, Check key and the list of models send your AI key to the provider it belongs to, and nothing of your work.

Nothing else is sent or fetched. The manual says the same in more detail.

The privacy page says what Idiograph and this website keep about you, and for how long.
