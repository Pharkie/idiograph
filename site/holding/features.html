IPA, step by step

IPA's own stepsThree screens follow Smith, Flowers and Larkin's (2022) steps: experiential statements, then PETs for each participant, then GETs across them.

On Experiential statements you read the transcript one participant passage at a time, with its exploratory notes, statements and quotes beside it. On PETs you group one participant's statements into personal experiential themes on a canvas. On GETs you gather every participant's PETs into group experiential themes. Idiograph uses the book's words, steps and tables (Smith, Flowers and Larkin 2022).

Experiential statements, passage by passageRead the transcript one participant passage at a time and write its exploratory notes, experiential statements and quotes beside it.

The transcript, your statements and your exploratory notes stand in three columns. You choose their order, and you can hide, widen or stack each one. The interviewer's passages show as context and can carry notes of their own. A small map in the corner shows where you are in a long transcript. Each statement is saved into the participant's workbook as you go.

Quotes from the participant's wordsSelect words in the passage to make a statement's quote. The transcript marks where you took it.

Each experiential statement has one quote. A quote can join several pieces of the same passage with an ellipsis. It can also take in the participant's passage just before or after, when only the interviewer speaks between them, and it then cites a time range. Words you select can also start a new statement with its quote. On the boards, you choose whether the cards show quotes Off, Short or Full.

PETs on a canvasDrag a participant's statements on a canvas into PETs, sub-PETs and groups. It works like sorting cards on a table.

Statements not yet placed and those set aside wait in a sidebar. You can stack, align and spread cards, or auto-arrange them as a grid, a column or a row. A PET or sub-PET can carry a description under its title, and sticky notes hold your own thoughts. The Inspector shows a card's passage and quote, and a double-click edits a statement in place.

Several boards for one participantTry different groupings of the same participant on boards side by side, and mark the one you are going with as Current.

A participant can have as many named boards as you like. Each new one starts fresh, as a copy or as a duplicate. Each board keeps its own History of revisions (see Finding, editing and undoing). The Current board is the one the GETs take.

PETs as a tableSwitch any board between the canvas and a table of PETs, and work in either.

The Table view does what the canvas does. You can select rows, group them, drag several at once, set aside and put back. The menus and keys are the same. Its Export menu prints and saves the table (see Exports and print).

GETs across participantsGather every participant's PETs into group experiential themes on one board. Each participant has a colour of their own.

A participant's PETs come in from their Current board, and a change to them later shows as changed. The GET table gives each GET a row for every participant with a key quote and your note for that row. The audit trail traces each GET back for every participant to the PET, the statement, the quote and the transcript passage. Idiograph checks each GET's count against a minimum, which is Smith's (2011) band unless you change it. A setting limits each PET to one GET.

Guidance beside each stepEach step has the method's guidance beside it, to open when you want it. There is guidance for statements, PETs and GETs, and in a thematic analysis project for coding, the code list and themes.

The statements guidance has eleven lines. Each one opens two short examples, Less this and More this. The PETs guidance covers how many PETs, grouping, titles, what to set aside and what to ask before finishing. The GETs guidance is the book's rules for a GET. Each is a plain text file beside the app, and you can edit it.

Nudges as you write a statementWhile you write a statement, optional prompts flag one that runs long or opens on "She" or "The".

The nudges are soft: nothing is blocked or scored. They flag a statement past the project's statement length (15 words unless you change it), one that opens on its subject or an article, a quoted fragment, a semicolon, a dash or an arrow, and "reads as" phrasing. Each project has its own nudges, and you turn each one on or off in the statements screen's View. A new project starts with them all off.

The book's labels and tablesPETs are lettered A, B, C as in the book's Box 5.6, and sub-PETs A1 and A2. If you prefer, they can be numbered PET 1 and 1a instead.

The labels are the same on the screen, in print, in Word and in the Markdown draft. You choose them for each project. The table layouts follow the book's tables of PETs and GETs.

Marking work completeMark a participant's statements, PETs and the GETs, and the Trajectories, complete, and see each participant's progress on the project's page.

Each participant's picture on Home and on the project's page has rings for how far their statements, PETs and LPETs have got, and a tooltip gives the numbers and whether each is marked complete. A project you have finished with can be archived in Project settings, and it moves to its own section on Home. In a project with timepoints each timepoint is marked on its own. Work in rounds paces a long transcript in rounds of ten minutes or a length you set, and sets a number of rounds a day. It is off in each project until you turn it on in the statements screen's View.

Participant demographicsSet categories for the project, such as age range or gender, and choose each participant's values. They show with the participant's name.

Each participant can have a picture, a label and a pen portrait. Each demographic category is a list of values or free words, and Add category offers a starting set. The values can show on the project's page, the GETs and Trajectories, and in the GET table's participants table.

Not plannedCharts and word cloudsIdiograph has no code counts by group, charts or word clouds of the kind mixed-methods tools have.

Bringing your work in

Transcripts in the shapes you haveBring in a transcript from Teams or Zoom (VTT), SRT, plain text, Word (.docx), CSV or Excel, and it becomes the participant's workbook.

A preview shows the first passages as they will come in. Each speaker is marked Interviewer, Participant or Other speaker. Idiograph reads timecodes in any common form, including ones with frames. A Word table, CSV or Excel file shows a chooser on every column. The original file is kept untouched in the participant's Source folder.

Work you have already doneColumns of exploratory notes, experiential statements and quotes come in on their passages, so a participant can open ready for PETs.

A column headed Notes, Comments, Memo or similar is read as exploratory notes, and a Quote column pairs each quote with the statement on the same line. A Word table laid out as the IPA book lays one out brings its notes in beside each passage. Notes on the interviewer's passages become interviewer notes.

Projects from NVivo, ATLAS.ti and MAXQDABring in a REFI-QDA project (.qdpx) exported from NVivo, ATLAS.ti or MAXQDA. Each text becomes a participant's interview.

Codes come in as experiential statements with their quotations as quotes, or as exploratory notes. Comments, uncoded quotations, memos and recordings come in as you choose, and code groups can make a draft PETs board. Variables come in as demographic categories if you tick Demographics. PDF documents, pictures, free memos, links and networks do not come in, and the import card says so. The card takes a file of up to 256 MB.

A new project and its transcriptsNew project sets the project's name, research question and method. Then you add the study's transcripts, give each a pseudonym, and Idiograph makes the participants' folders and workbooks.

Files under one pseudonym become one participant with an interview each. Add transcripts brings in later interviews or new participants. Project settings also keeps the interviewer's name and your reflexivity statement for the project. A project folder already on your computer opens with Open project.

Recordings beside the transcriptAdd the interview's recording (.m4a, .mp3, .wav) and play each passage from its timecode.

The player follows the passage you are on and jumps five seconds back or forward. An offset lines up a recording that starts a little differently from the transcript's times. Idiograph names a recording it copies in by the participant's pseudonym.

Open a shared copyOpen a project someone has sent you as one .idiograph file.

It opens as a copy in a new folder, and Idiograph marks it as a copy. A double-click on the file opens it once Idiograph is installed.

Sharing and working together

Share a copySend a whole project or chosen participants to another Idiograph as one .idiograph file.

You choose whether PETs and recordings go, and whether every board goes or only the Current one. The file holds participants' words, so send it only as your ethics approval allows. Idiograph does not track changes between copies.

A blind copy for a second analystSend a participant without your PETs, so a colleague can make their own from the same statements.

In Share a copy, choose the participant and untick their PETs. The GETs and your grouping stay behind.

Boards back from a colleagueWhen a colleague sends your copy back, add their boards to your project as new boards beside yours.

Each board keeps its cards where your colleague put them. A statement you have reworded since shows your wording and is marked changed. Nothing else in the project changes.

ExperimentalWorking togetherShare a project with colleagues. Each of you works on your own participants and reviews the others'.

Each participant has one owner, and only their computer changes that participant. Changes go end-to-end encrypted through a relay, or through a folder you already share such as OneDrive or Dropbox. Press Invite others on a project, or Join a project on Home, where a colleague pastes the link or twelve words and Idiograph makes the project's folder. You both check six digits. Invites you have sent wait under People until someone answers, and you can cancel one. The project's name, research question, method and each participant's profile reach everyone on the team, while your own reflexivity statement and interviewer name stay yours. It is off in every project until you turn it on.

ExperimentalComments and suggestionsComment on a colleague's passages, statements and themes, or suggest a wording or a title. They accept or decline each suggestion.

A review goes as one message with all its comments and suggestions. Nothing changes on the owner's computer until they answer, and History records who suggested what. A colleague can also send a whole board. It comes in as the owner's next board.

PlannedCoding apart, then comparingIdiograph cannot yet set two analysts' work on one participant side by side, or hide one analyst's statements while the other works. A blind copy comes closest.
Not plannedTwo people on one participant at onceWorking together is experimental, and in it each participant has one owner.

The app

Mac, Windows and LinuxIdiograph runs on a Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or later), on Windows, and on Linux.

The Mac app needs macOS 12 or later. On Windows it installs for you alone and needs no administrator's password. For Linux there is a .deb for Ubuntu, Debian and Mint and an AppImage for other Linux, both for 64-bit Intel or AMD. The installers are not signed yet, so your computer asks once before you first open Idiograph.

Free and open sourceIdiograph is free and needs no account or subscription. Its code is open under the GNU AGPL v3.

Anyone can read, change and share the code on GitHub, under the same licence.

Updates itselfIdiograph checks once a day for a newer version and installs it when you relaunch. You can turn this off.

Each update is signed, and Idiograph never installs one whose signature does not hold. You choose whether it downloads without asking.

Several windowsOpen a participant's transcript in one window and a board in another, on two screens if you have them.

The two keep each other up to date as you work.

Keyboard and screen readerYou can work the app by keyboard, and a card lists its shortcuts. Its cards, menus and headings are named for screen readers.

It has not yet been tested by people who use a screen reader day to day.

Light, dark and text sizeChoose light, dark or the computer's own appearance, and make text larger or smaller with Cmd and plus or minus.

Every screen and print view follows the choice.

Language and formatsThe app is in British or American English. Dates, times and numbers follow your computer unless you choose otherwise.

You set each project's timecodes as 75:12, 1:15:12 or 01:15:12, and they look the same everywhere. Other languages are prepared for but not yet available.

Demo projectsTry everything on four finished demo projects. Their participants are invented.

Living with a dog is an IPA study of three participants, and a second demo codes the same interviews by reflexive thematic analysis. First year on an allotment is a longitudinal IPA study of three participants at three timepoints. Working from home is a reflexive thematic analysis study of six participants. When Idiograph brings a newer version of a demo, Home tells you and puts it in place only when you ask. Reset demo puts a demo back as it was.

Send feedbackThe bug button on every screen sends a note to the developer from inside the app.

It carries your words, an optional reply address, the app's version, your system and the screen's name. It carries nothing from your projects.

Longitudinal IPA

TimepointsLongitudinal IPA interviews each participant more than once (Farr and Nizza 2019). Idiograph keeps each timepoint's statements and PETs apart.

When you make a Longitudinal IPA project, you set its timepoints and name each one, such as "T1, Before the season". You can add more later in Project settings. Each participant keeps one workbook. It has a sheet for each interview at each timepoint. You work on statements and PETs and mark them complete one timepoint at a time. The project's page puts the LGETs and Trajectories at the top, above the participants.

PETs at each timepointEach timepoint of a participant has its own PETs boards.

A timepoint's canvas holds only that timepoint's statements. Each timepoint has its own boards, its own Current board and its own PETs complete.

LPETsBring one participant's PETs from every timepoint onto one board and group them into longitudinal PETs.

Each card is a PET or sub-PET from one timepoint, marked "PET · T1". A group over cards is an LPET. When a timepoint's PETs change, the board shows the change, as GETs do. Statements cite their timepoint, as in "T1, 35:55". The AI features do not work on LPETs.

LGETsGather every participant's LPETs into longitudinal group themes.

In a project with two or more timepoints, LGETs take the place of GETs. They use the same canvas, and each LPET shows in its participant's colour. The AI features do not work on LGETs.

TrajectoriesPlace each participant on a trajectory you name, and see how their LPETs change across the timepoints. Each timepoint shows a quote and a short state.

Each trajectory is a band with your own title, colour and icon, such as Rising, Mixed or Falling. A participant shows a picture or initial, a label of your own and their demographics. Each of their LPETs is a row with its key quote over its state at every timepoint. You move participants by dragging or with the keys.

Not plannedAI suggestions on LPETs and LGETsThe AI features suggest titles for PETs and GETs only. An AI agent of your own can work on LPETs and LGETs.

Reflexive thematic analysis

Thematic analysis projectsWhen you make a project, you can choose Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis (2006, 2019, 2022) instead of IPA.

A thematic analysis project has three screens: Coding, Code list and Themes. Their names are Braun and Clarke's (2022) words. It has no PETs, timepoints or trajectories. Codebook approaches, framework analysis and coding reliability with a second coder are not part of it.

Coding, passage by passageRead each passage and give its extracts their codes. You can do it all from the keyboard.

Each extract is the participant's own words with its codes as chips under it. As you type a code, Idiograph suggests the project's codes, and quick keys give an extract a code you use often.

One code list for every participantSee every code with its extracts across all participants, and rename, describe or merge codes there.

A code is a label many extracts share. The code list is kept in one file at the project's root, so every participant's coding uses the same codes.

Themes boardBuild the thematic map on a board from every participant's codes.

Themes and subthemes hold codes, as GETs hold PETs. The theme table lists each theme with its codes and extracts, and the audit trail traces a theme back to the passages.

Braun and Clarke's (2022) guidanceThe Coding screen, the Code list and the Themes board each have guidance beside them. It follows Braun and Clarke (2022).

The coding guidance has eleven lines, and each has a Less this and a More this. The code list guidance has eight. The themes guidance covers what a theme is, generating, reviewing, defining and naming themes, and the thematic map.

Thematic analysis demosTwo demos use reflexive thematic analysis. One codes the IPA demo's three invented interviews, so you can compare the two methods on the same words. The other, Working from home, has six invented participants and interviews of its own.

Home's Demo projects holds all four demos. Reset demo puts any of them back as it was.

Not plannedCodebook methods and agreement scoresTemplate, framework and codebook analysis are other methods, and so is measuring agreement between coders. Idiograph does IPA and reflexive thematic analysis.

Exports and print

Tables for your supervisor and thesisPrint a participant's table of PETs, save it as a PDF, or write it as a Microsoft Word document in the book's layout.

Print and Save as PDF set the table on landscape A4. Every page's foot names the participant, the project, your name, Confidential, the date and the page. The Word document is portrait A4 in Word's own heading styles, so Word's navigation pane lists the PETs. It opens in Word, Pages and LibreOffice. Ticks let you include descriptions, the statements unplaced, set aside or stacked, and the board's name.

The GET table and audit trailPrint or save as PDF the GET table, the audit trail, or all of it in one document with each participant's table of PETs. The GET table also goes to Word.

The GET table gives each GET a row for every participant. The audit trail traces each GET for every participant from the passage to the quote, the statement and the PET. You choose every statement under each participant or their key quote only. The GET table and the LGET table go to Microsoft Word as the table of PETs does.

The canvas as a picture or PDFSave any board's canvas as a PNG picture or a one-page PDF, for a slide or a figure.

Every card is shown whole, and the picture is at twice the screen's sharpness. Print puts the canvas on one landscape page. Choose A3 in the print dialog for a larger one.

Statements as CSV, PETs as MarkdownWrite every statement of a participant to a CSV with its timestamp, quote and PET. Write the board's PETs as a Markdown draft.

The CSV opens in Excel or any spreadsheet. The Markdown draft lists each PET, sub-PET and group with their statements and quotes. A tick also writes the interviewer notes to a Markdown file.

Thematic analysis exportsPrint or save as PDF the table of themes, the audit trail or the full set. Write the table of themes to Word and the codes to CSV.

Each uses Braun and Clarke's (2022) words: Table of themes, Code, Extract, Theme. The Word document is portrait A4, as the PETs table's is.

Trajectories as PDF and WordPrint Trajectories, save them as a PDF or write them as a Microsoft Word document.

Each participant's note can be included, and so can the participants you have not placed on a trajectory. The file is saved at the project's root.

A participant's annotated transcriptWrite a participant's transcript with your notes and statements beside it, as a document next to their workbook.

Choose which to include: exploratory notes, experiential statements, interviewer notes and the pen portrait. In a thematic analysis project it is the coded transcript.

The code list as PDF, Word and CSVPrint the code list, save it as a PDF, write it to Microsoft Word, or write it to CSV with each code's extracts.

The code list has its own row of tools with Export. It is for a thematic analysis project.

Export as REFI-QDAExport a project, or chosen participants, as a REFI-QDA file (.qdpx) that NVivo, ATLAS.ti, MAXQDA and QualCoder open.

Each interview goes as a text with its speakers and times, each experiential statement as a code on its quote, exploratory notes as notes, and each PET, GET, LPET and LGET as a set. Recordings go if you tick them. Bring the file back into Idiograph and you get back the passages, statements, quotes, notes and all four kinds of board. Where the cards lay on a board does not come back.

The same pages on every computerIdiograph typesets every PDF and picture itself, so a printout looks the same on a Mac, Windows and Linux.

The pages are set in Charis SIL and Nunito Sans, two typefaces that come with the app. Nothing else needs to be installed.

Excel is the workbook itselfThere is no separate Excel export: each participant's workbook is an Excel file you can open at any time.

See Your data and confidentiality. When you bring in a transcript in another format, Idiograph makes it into an Excel workbook.

Your data and confidentiality

Your Excel workbook, on your computerIdiograph keeps each participant's transcript, notes, statements and quotes in an Excel workbook in a folder on your computer.

The workbook has the columns Name, Timestamp, Experiential statements, Transcript, Exploratory notes and Quote, with one passage on each row. Excel or any other tool can read it. Cells Idiograph does not change keep their formatting. While Excel has the workbook open, Idiograph does not write to it and tells you so. Boards, the GETs and the project's settings are plain JSON files beside the workbooks.

No account and no cloudIdiograph needs no account or subscription, and participants' words never leave your computer unless you send them.

A project is a folder you choose, and you back it up like any other. The page loads nothing from the internet. The next item says what can leave your computer and when.

What leaves your computer, and whenYour work leaves your computer only when you turn on a feature and use it: AI requests, Working together's encrypted messages, or an AI agent you allow. The daily update check sends no work at all.

Once a day Idiograph asks GitHub for a newer version. GitHub sees your internet address and version number but none of your work. Idiograph asks you before its first check, and you can turn it off in Settings. A feedback note carries your words, the app's version, your system and the screen's name. It carries nothing from your projects. With AI features on, the passage or board you use an AI control on goes to the provider you chose. With a model on your own computer nothing goes out. Working together sends end-to-end encrypted messages through a relay or a shared folder, and an AI agent sends what it reads to its own provider.

Real names replaced as transcripts come inKeep a list of real names and their pseudonyms, and every transcript coming into the project has them replaced on the way in.

Idiograph finds names as whole words, and initials too. You review each replacement before it is written. The list can also be checked against transcripts already in. It is kept in one file in the project's folder, and it never goes into a workbook, History or a copy you share.

Copies kept before big changesBefore a rename, a trim, a names replacement or a new interview is written, Idiograph keeps a copy of the files as they were.

The copies go in the participant's `_Archive` folder. Each is named for the change and the date. A deleted board is kept there too.

Remove a project, keep its filesWhen you remove a project from Idiograph, its folder stays as it is, unless you tick the box to move it to the Bin.

If you move it to the Bin, the Finder's Put Back restores it.

Participants by pseudonymThe screens, the prints and every copy you share name participants by pseudonym and number, as in "P8 Wren".

Rename changes a participant's pseudonym everywhere at once: in the workbook, the boards and the GETs. Idiograph keeps a copy of each file first. A recording copied in is named by pseudonym and timepoint.

Finding, editing and undoing

Search everythingCmd+F (Ctrl+F) finds words in passages, notes, statements, quotes, PETs and GETs, in this participant, this project or every project.

Search finds every word you type, in any order and in its other forms, so "sleep" finds "slept". Whole word and Match case are ticks, and words in quotation marks are found together. A result opens where it is.

Find and replaceCorrect a word or a name in a participant's whole transcript at once, and in your statements, quotes and notes too.

Find and replace (Cmd+Shift+F) runs over every sheet of the participant's workbook. It matches your words exactly, not as a pattern, and has Whole word and Match case. Replace all changes the transcript, statements, quotes, exploratory notes and interviewer notes, and the boards follow. One Undo takes it all back. An AI agent can replace across every participant at once, once you say yes.

Correct the transcriptFix a passage's words, a timecode or a speaker in place, without opening Excel.

Edit a passage, correct its timecode, split words said by another speaker into a passage of their own, and cut the consent talk and goodbyes from either end. Each change is recorded and can be undone.

Undo and RedoCmd+Z undoes your last steps on the statements screen and on every board, several levels deep.

On a board that includes moves, groups, renames, set-asides and sticky notes. On the statements screen it includes quotes, corrections, splits and trims.

History of each boardEvery board keeps its earlier versions. Idiograph saves one every fifteen minutes and whenever you ask, and you can restore any of them.

Idiograph also keeps a revision before a restore, a copy or an AI agent's change. A version made by hand keeps the name you gave it.

The project's historyIdiograph records each change to a project's settings, each rename and each completion: when, what, and whether you or an AI agent made it.

Project history in the project's menu lists them newest first. A shared copy does not carry it.

Rename a participantChange a participant's pseudonym, and every workbook, board and GET follows.

The card lists what will change before anything is written, and keeps a copy of each file first. The folder can be renamed too.

Interviewer notesKeep notes on the interviewer's own passages, apart from the exploratory notes.

They are saved in the workbook and can be exported with the statements.

AI features and agents

ExperimentalAI featuresOptional AI suggestions use your own key or a model on your own computer. They are off on a new install and hidden until you turn them on.

There are eight providers: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral at its EU address, Azure OpenAI, another service by its address, and Ollama or LM Studio on your computer. Ollama and LM Studio send nothing out. Before you turn AI features on, Settings explains why they are not advised for most analysis. The board and History mark whatever an AI made.

ExperimentalSuggested statementsWith AI on, ask for suggested experiential statements for a passage. You take, change or ignore each one.

Nothing is written until you take a suggestion. The prompt can be read and changed in Settings.

ExperimentalAI on the boardsWith AI on, a PET or a GET can get three suggested titles.

A title goes on only when you pick one. In a thematic analysis project the AI suggests codes for an extract. LPETs and LGETs have none.

ExperimentalYour own AI agentsAI agents you already use, such as Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI, can work on a project you allow. They use Idiograph's command line or MCP server.

A project allows agents only once you tick it, and what an agent reads goes to its provider. Agents work under the app's rules and try each change as a dry run first. Deleting, replacing across participants or sending a project out waits for your yes in the app. Idiograph marks participants' words as words to read, so an agent does not take them as instructions. Project history's By an agent lists each agent's changes by run. You can undo a whole run or one change, and undoing a run takes away everything the run added, including the transcripts it brought in.

Your own tables of PETsAn AI agent of your own can turn a table of PETs or LPETs you made in Word or Excel into a board, through Idiograph's command line.

Each row is matched to the participant's statement by its words, and a row that does not match is added or skipped as you say. There is no screen for this in the app yet.

The command lineEverything the app does to a project can also be done from a command line, for scripts and agents.

It shares the app's rules, History and undo, and every write can be tried as a dry run first.
