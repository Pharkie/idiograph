IPA, step by step

IPA's own stepsThree screens follow Smith, Flowers and Larkin's steps: experiential statements, then PETs for each participant, then GETs across them.

Experiential statements is the transcript read one participant passage at a time, with its exploratory notes, statements and quotes beside it. PETs groups one participant's statements into personal experiential themes on a canvas. GETs gathers every participant's PETs into group experiential themes. The words, the steps and the tables are the book's (Smith, Flowers and Larkin 2022).

Experiential statements, passage by passageRead the transcript one participant passage at a time and write its exploratory notes, experiential statements and quotes beside it.

The transcript, your statements and your exploratory notes stand in three columns, in the order you choose, and each column can be hidden, widened or stacked. The interviewer's passages show as context and can carry notes of their own. A small map in the corner shows where you are in a long transcript. Each statement is saved into the participant's workbook as you go.

Quotes from the participant's wordsSelect words in the passage to make a statement's quote, marked in the transcript where it was taken.

Each experiential statement has one quote, which can be made of several pieces of the same passage, joined with an ellipsis. A quote can also take in the participant's passage just before or after, with only the interviewer between, and then cites a time range. Words you select can also start a new statement with its quote. Quotes show on the cards on the boards, Off, Short or Full.

PETs on a canvasDrag a participant's statements into PETs, sub-PETs and groups on a canvas, as you would with cards on a table.

Statements not yet placed and those set aside wait in a sidebar. Cards can be stacked, aligned, spread, or auto-arranged as a grid, a column or a row. A PET or sub-PET can carry a description under its title, and sticky notes hold your own thoughts. The Inspector shows a card's passage and quote, and a double-click edits a statement in place.

Several boards for one participantTry different groupings of the same participant on boards side by side, and mark the one you are going with as Current.

A participant can have as many named boards as you like, each a fresh start, a copy or a duplicate. Each board keeps its own History of revisions (see Finding, editing and undoing). The Current board is the one the GETs take.

PETs as a tableSwitch any board between the canvas and a table of PETs, and work in either.

The Table view does what the canvas does: select rows, group them, drag several at once, set aside and put back, with the same menus and keys. Its Export menu prints and saves the table (see Exports and print).

GETs across participantsGather every participant's PETs into group experiential themes on one board, each participant in a colour of their own.

A participant's PETs come in from their Current board, and a change to them later shows as changed. The GET table gives each GET a row for every participant with a key quote and your note for that row. The audit trail traces each GET back for every participant to the PET, the statement, the quote and the transcript passage. A GET's count is set against a minimum, Smith's band by default, and one GET per PET is a setting.

Guidance beside each stepThe method's guidance sits beside each step, to open when you want it: for statements, PETs, GETs and, in a thematic analysis project, for codes and themes.

The statements guidance has eleven lines, each opening two short examples, Less this and More this. The PETs guidance covers how many PETs, grouping, titles, what to set aside and what to ask before finishing. The GETs guidance is the book's rules for a GET. Each is a plain text file beside the app, so it can be edited.

Nudges as you write a statementOptional prompts flag a statement that runs long or opens on "She" or "The", while you are still writing it.

The nudges are soft: nothing is blocked or scored. They flag a statement past the project's statement length (15 words unless you change it), one that opens on its subject or an article, a quoted fragment, a semicolon, a dash or an arrow, and "reads as" phrasing. Each can be turned off in Settings.

The book's labels and tablesPETs are lettered A, B, C as in the book's Box 5.6, with sub-PETs A1 and A2, or numbered PET 1 and 1a if you prefer.

The labels are the same on the screen, in print, in Word and in the Markdown draft. The choice belongs to the project. The table layouts follow the book's tables of PETs and GETs.

Marking work completeMark a participant's statements, PETs and the GETs complete, and see each participant's progress on the project's page.

Each participant's row shows how many passages are decided, "64 of 207 passages", and whether statements and PETs are complete, with the date in a tooltip. In a project with timepoints each timepoint is marked on its own. An optional Work in rounds setting paces a long transcript in ten-minute rounds with a daily target. It is off unless you turn it on.

Not yetParticipant attributes and chartsNo demographic variables, code counts by group, charts or word clouds, as mixed-methods tools have.

Bringing your work in

Transcripts in the shapes you haveBring in a transcript from Teams or Zoom (VTT), SRT, plain text, Word (.docx), CSV or Excel, and it becomes the participant's workbook.

A preview shows the first passages as they will land. Each speaker is marked Interviewer, Participant or Other speaker, and timecodes are read in any common form, with frames if needed. A Word table, CSV or Excel file shows a chooser on every column. The original file is kept untouched in the participant's Source folder.

Work you have already doneColumns of exploratory notes, experiential statements and quotes come in on their passages, so a participant can open ready for PETs.

A column headed Notes, Comments, Memo or similar is read as exploratory notes, and a Quote column pairs each quote with the statement on the same line. A Word table laid out as the IPA book lays one out brings its notes in beside each passage. Notes on the interviewer's passages become interviewer notes.

Projects from NVivo, ATLAS.ti and MAXQDABring in a REFI-QDA project (.qdpx) exported from NVivo, ATLAS.ti or MAXQDA, each text a participant's interview.

Codes come in as experiential statements with their quotations as quotes, or as exploratory notes. Comments, uncoded quotations, memos and recordings come in as you choose, and code groups can make a draft PETs board. PDF documents, pictures, variables, free memos, links and networks do not come in, and the import card says so. Up to 256 MB through the card.

A new project from a folder of transcriptsDrop a study's transcripts on New project, give each a pseudonym, and Idiograph makes the participants' folders and workbooks.

Files under one pseudonym become one participant with an interview each. Add transcripts brings in later interviews or new participants. A project folder already on your computer opens with Open project.

Recordings beside the transcriptAdd the interview's recording (.m4a, .mp3, .wav) and play each passage from its timecode.

The player follows the passage you are on, with five-second jumps back and forward. An offset lines up a recording that starts a little differently from the transcript's times. A copied recording is named by the participant's pseudonym, never by its own file name.

Open a shared copyOpen a project someone has sent you as one .idiograph file.

It opens as a copy in a new folder, marked copy. A double-click on the file opens it once Idiograph is installed.

Through an agentYour own tables of PETsA table of PETs or LPETs you made in Word or Excel can become a board, through an AI agent and the command line.

Each row is matched to the participant's statement by its words, and a row that does not match is added or skipped as you say. There is no screen for this in the app yet.

Not yetVideo, PDF and image sourcesOnly transcripts are analysed, with their audio beside them.
Not yetOlder document formats.doc, .odt, .rtf, .xls and PDF transcripts are not read (.docx, .xlsx, CSV, VTT, SRT and plain text are).

Sharing and working together

Share a copySend a whole project, or chosen participants, to another Idiograph as one .idiograph file.

You choose whether PETs and recordings go, and every board or only the Current one. The file holds participants' words, so it should travel as your ethics approval allows. Idiograph does not track changes between copies.

A blind copy for a second analystSend a participant without your PETs, so a colleague can make their own from the same statements.

In Share a copy, choose the participant and untick their PETs. The GETs and your grouping stay behind.

Boards back from a colleagueWhen a colleague sends your copy back, add their boards to your project as new boards beside yours.

Each board keeps its cards where your colleague put them, and statements you have reworded since show your wording, marked changed. Nothing else in the project changes.

ExperimentalWorking togetherShare a project with colleagues, each working on their own participants and reviewing each other's.

Each participant has one owner, and only their computer changes that participant. Changes travel end to end encrypted, through a relay or a folder you already share, such as OneDrive or Dropbox. A colleague joins with a link or twelve words, and you both check six digits on a call. It is off in every project until you turn it on.

ExperimentalComments and suggestionsComment on a colleague's passages, statements and themes, or suggest a wording or a title, which they accept or decline.

A review goes as one message with all its comments and suggestions. Nothing changes on the owner's computer until they answer, and History records who suggested what. A colleague can also send a whole board, which comes in as the owner's next board.

Not yetCoding apart, then comparingNo side-by-side comparison of two analysts' work on one participant, or hiding one analyst's statements while the other works (a blind copy comes closest).
Not yetTwo people on one participant at onceIn Working together, which is experimental, each participant has one owner.

The app

Mac, Windows and LinuxIdiograph runs on a Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or later), on Windows, and on Linux.

The Mac app needs macOS 12 or later. Windows installs for you alone, with no administrator's password. Linux has a .deb for Ubuntu, Debian and Mint and an AppImage for other Linux, on 64-bit Intel or AMD. The installers are not signed yet, so the first open asks once.

Free and open sourceIdiograph is free, with no account or subscription, and its code is open under the GNU AGPL v3.

Anyone can read, change and share the code on GitHub, under the same licence.

Updates itselfIdiograph checks once a day for a newer version and installs it when you relaunch, or not at all if you turn it off.

Each update is signed, and one whose signature does not hold is never installed. You choose whether it downloads without asking.

Several windowsOpen a participant's transcript in one window and a board in another, on two screens if you have them.

The two keep each other up to date as you work.

Keyboard and screen readerThe app can be worked by keyboard, with a card of its shortcuts, and its cards, menus and headings are named for screen readers.

It has not yet been tested by people who use a screen reader day to day.

Light, dark and text sizeLight, dark or the computer's own appearance, and larger or smaller text with Cmd and plus or minus.

Every screen and print view follows the choice.

Language and formatsThe app is in English, British or American, and dates, times and numbers follow your computer unless you choose.

Timecodes are set for each project, 75:12, 1:15:12 or 01:15:12, and agree everywhere. Other languages are prepared for but not yet available.

Demo projectsA finished demo study of three invented participants, in IPA and in thematic analysis, lets you try everything on nobody's words.

Reset demo puts it back as it was.

Send feedbackThe bug button on every screen sends a note to the developer from inside the app.

It carries your words, an optional reply address, the app's version, your system and the screen's name, and nothing from your projects.

Longitudinal IPA

TimepointsInterview each participant more than once and keep each timepoint's statements and PETs apart, as longitudinal IPA does (Farr and Nizza 2019).

Project settings sets how many timepoints and names each, "T1, Before the season". Each participant keeps one workbook, with a sheet for each interview at each timepoint. Statements and PETs are worked and marked complete one timepoint at a time. With one timepoint nothing longitudinal shows.

PETs at each timepointEach timepoint of a participant has its own PETs boards.

A timepoint's canvas holds only that timepoint's statements, with its own boards, Current board and PETs complete.

LPETsBring one participant's PETs from every timepoint onto one board and group them into longitudinal PETs.

Each card is a PET or sub-PET of one timepoint, "PET · T1", and a group over cards is an LPET. A change to a timepoint's PETs comes in as a change, as on GETs. Statements cite their timepoint, "T1, 35:55". The AI features do not reach LPETs.

LGETsGather every participant's LPETs into longitudinal group themes.

In a project of two or more timepoints LGETs take the place of GETs: the same canvas, each LPET in its participant's colour. The AI features do not reach LGETs.

TrajectoriesPlace each participant on a trajectory you name, and see their LPETs change across the timepoints, with a quote and a short state at each.

Each trajectory is a band with your title, colour and icon, Rising, Mixed or Falling for example. A participant shows a picture or initial, a label of your own and tags. Each of their LPETs is a row with its key quote over its state at every timepoint. Participants move by dragging or by keys.

Not yetLPETs and LGETs in REFI-QDA, and AI help on themThe REFI-QDA export carries PETs and GETs, and the AI features stop at PETs and GETs.

Reflexive thematic analysis

Thematic analysis projectsA project can follow Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis instead of IPA, chosen when the project is made.

A thematic analysis project has three screens, Coding, Code list and Themes, in Braun and Clarke's words. It has no PETs, timepoints or trajectories. Codebook approaches, framework analysis and coding reliability with a second coder are not part of it.

Coding, passage by passageRead each passage and give its extracts their codes, from the keyboard if you like.

Each extract is the participant's own words with its codes as chips under it. Adding a code suggests the project's codes as you type, and quick keys give an extract a code you use often.

One code list for every participantSee every code with its extracts across all participants, and rename, describe or merge codes there.

A code is a label many extracts share. The code list is kept in one file at the project's root, so every participant's coding uses the same codes.

Themes boardBuild the thematic map on a board from every participant's codes.

Themes and subthemes hold codes, as GETs hold PETs. The theme table lists each theme with its codes and extracts, and the audit trail traces a theme back to the passages.

Braun and Clarke's guidanceGuidance for coding and for themes, from Braun and Clarke, sits beside the Coding screen and the Themes board.

The coding guidance has eleven lines, each with Less this and More this. The themes guidance covers what a theme is, generating, reviewing, defining and naming themes, and the thematic map.

A thematic analysis demoA second demo codes the same three invented interviews by thematic analysis, so the two methods can be compared on the same words.

Home's Demo projects holds both. Reset demo puts either back as it was.

Not yetCodebook methods and agreement scoresTemplate, framework and codebook analysis, and agreement between coders, are other methods: Idiograph does IPA and reflexive thematic analysis.

Exports and print

Tables for your supervisor and thesisPrint a participant's table of PETs, save it as a PDF, or write it as a Microsoft Word document in the book's layout.

Print and Save as PDF set the table on landscape A4, every page's foot naming the participant, the project, your name, Confidential, the date and the page. The Word document is portrait A4 in Word's own heading styles, so its navigation pane lists the PETs, and it opens in Word, Pages and LibreOffice. Ticks choose descriptions, the statements not placed, set aside or stacked, and whether the board's name shows.

The GET table and audit trailPrint or save as PDF the GET table, the audit trail, or all of it in one document with each participant's table of PETs.

The GET table gives each GET a row for every participant. The audit trail traces each GET for every participant from the passage to the quote, the statement and the PET. You choose every statement under each participant or their key quote only. The GET table has no Word version yet.

The canvas as a picture or PDFSave any board's canvas as a PNG picture or a one-page PDF, for a slide or a figure.

Every card is shown whole, and the picture is at twice the screen's sharpness. Print puts the canvas on one landscape page, A3 if you choose it in the print dialog.

Statements as CSV, PETs as MarkdownWrite every statement of a participant to a CSV, with its timestamp, quote and PET, and the board's PETs as a Markdown draft.

The CSV opens in Excel or any spreadsheet. The Markdown draft lists each PET, sub-PET and group with their statements and quotes. A tick also writes the interviewer notes to a Markdown file.

Thematic analysis exportsPrint or save as PDF the table of themes, the audit trail or the full set, write the table of themes to Word, and the codes to CSV.

Each uses Braun and Clarke's words: Table of themes, Code, Extract, Theme. The Word document is portrait A4, as the PETs table's is.

Export as REFI-QDAExport a project, or chosen participants, as a REFI-QDA file (.qdpx) that NVivo, ATLAS.ti, MAXQDA and QualCoder open.

Each interview goes as a text with its speakers and times, each experiential statement as a code on its quote, exploratory notes as notes, and each PET and GET as a set. Recordings go if you tick them. Brought back into Idiograph, the file gives back the passages, statements, quotes, notes, PETs and GETs. LPETs and LGETs are not in the file yet, nor where cards lay on a board.

The same pages on every computerEvery PDF and picture is typeset by Idiograph itself, so a printout looks the same on a Mac, Windows and Linux.

The pages are set in two typefaces the app carries, Charis SIL and Nunito Sans. Nothing else needs to be installed.

Excel is the workbook itselfThere is no separate Excel export: each participant's workbook is an Excel file you can open at any time.

See Your data and confidentiality. A transcript in another shape is made into an Excel workbook as it comes in.

Not yetThe GET table in WordIt prints and saves as a PDF only.

Your data and confidentiality

Your Excel workbook, on your computerEach participant's transcript, notes, statements and quotes are kept in an Excel workbook in a folder on your computer.

The workbook has the columns Name, Timestamp, Experiential statements, Transcript, Exploratory notes and Quote, one passage a row, so Excel or any other tool can read it. Cells Idiograph does not change keep their formatting as they were. It will not write while Excel has the workbook open, and says so. Boards, the GETs and the project's settings are plain JSON files beside the workbooks.

No account and no cloudIdiograph needs no account or subscription, and participants' words never leave your computer unless you send them.

A project is a folder you choose, which you back up like any other. The page loads nothing from the internet. What can leave, and when, is in the next item.

What leaves your computer, and whenNothing of your work leaves unless you turn a feature on and use it: AI requests, Working together's encrypted messages, or an AI agent you allow, and the daily update check sends no work at all.

Once a day Idiograph asks GitHub for a newer version, which sees your internet address and version number, never your work. It asks before its first check, and Settings can turn it off. A feedback note carries your words, the app's version, your system and the screen's name, nothing from your projects. With AI features on, the passage or board you press an AI control on goes to the provider you chose, nothing with a local model. Working together sends end-to-end encrypted messages through a relay or a shared folder, and an AI agent sends what it reads to its own provider.

Real names replaced as transcripts come inKeep a list of real names and their pseudonyms, and every transcript coming into the project has them replaced on the way in.

Names are found as whole words, initials too, and you review each replacement before it is written. The list can also be checked against transcripts already in. It is kept in one file in the project's folder, never in a workbook, History or a copy you share.

Copies kept before big changesBefore a rename, a trim, a names replacement or a new interview is written, Idiograph keeps a copy of the files as they were.

The copies go in the participant's `_Archive` folder, named for the change and the date. A deleted board is kept there too.

Remove a project, keep its filesRemoving a project from Idiograph leaves its folder as it is, unless you tick to move it to the Bin.

Moved to the Bin, the Finder's Put Back brings it back.

Participants by pseudonymThe screens, the prints and every copy you share name participants by pseudonym and number, "P8 Wren".

Rename changes a participant's pseudonym everywhere at once, the workbook, the boards and the GETs, with a copy kept first. A recording copied in is named by pseudonym and timepoint.

Finding, editing and undoing

Search everythingCmd+F (Ctrl+F) finds words in passages, notes, statements, quotes, PETs and GETs, in this participant, this project or every project.

Every word typed is found in any order, with its other forms, so "sleep" finds "slept". Whole word and Match case are ticks, and words in quotation marks are found together. A result opens where it is.

Find and replaceCorrect a word or a name in a participant's whole transcript at once, and in your statements, quotes and notes too.

Find and replace (Cmd+Shift+F) runs over every sheet of the participant's workbook, literally, never as a pattern, with Whole word and Match case. Replace all changes the transcript, statements, quotes, exploratory notes and interviewer notes, and the boards follow. One Undo takes it all back. Across every participant at once is for an AI agent, with your yes.

Correct the transcriptFix a passage's words, a timecode or a speaker in place, without opening Excel.

Edit a passage, correct its timecode, split words said by another speaker into a passage of their own, and cut the consent talk and goodbyes from either end. Each change is recorded and can be undone.

Undo and RedoCmd+Z undoes your last steps on the statements screen and on every board, several levels deep.

On a board that includes moves, groups, renames, set-asides and sticky notes. On the statements screen it includes quotes, corrections, splits and trims.

History of each boardEvery board keeps its earlier versions, saved every fifteen minutes and whenever you ask, and any one can be restored.

A revision is kept before a restore, a copy or an AI agent's change too. A version made by hand keeps the name you gave it.

The project's historyEach change to a project's settings, a rename or a completion is recorded with when, what and who, you or an AI agent.

Project history, in the project's menu, lists them newest first. A shared copy does not carry it.

Rename a participantChange a participant's pseudonym, and every workbook, board and GET follows.

The card lists what will change before anything is written, and keeps a copy of each file first. The folder can be renamed too.

Interviewer notesKeep notes on the interviewer's own passages, apart from the exploratory notes.

They are saved in the workbook and can be exported with the statements.

AI features and agents

ExperimentalAI featuresOptional AI suggestions, off on a new install and hidden entirely until you turn them on, with your own key or a model on your own computer.

Eight providers: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral at its EU address, Azure OpenAI, another service by its address, and Ollama or LM Studio on your computer, which send nothing out. Settings explains why they are not advised for most analysis before they turn on. Whatever an AI made is marked as such on the board and in History.

ExperimentalSuggested statementsWith AI on, ask for suggested experiential statements for a passage, to take, change or ignore.

Nothing is written until you take a suggestion. The prompt can be read and changed in Settings.

ExperimentalAI on the boardsWith AI on, a PET or a GET can get three suggested titles.

A title goes on only when you pick one. In a thematic analysis project the AI suggests codes for an extract. LPETs and LGETs have none.

ExperimentalYour own AI agentsAI agents you already use, such as Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI, can work on a project you allow, through Idiograph's command line or MCP server.

A project allows agents only once you tick it, and what an agent reads goes to its provider. Agents work under the app's rules, with a dry run first. Deleting, replacing across participants or sending a project out waits for your yes in the app. Participants' words reach the agent marked as words to read, never as instructions.

The command lineEverything the app does to a project can also be done from a command line, for scripts and agents.

It shares the app's rules, History and undo, and every write can be tried as a dry run first.

Idiograph is for Mac, Windows and Linux computers.
