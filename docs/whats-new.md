# What's new in Idiograph

Newest first. Each version's notes are what the updater shows, eight lines at most, and
`packaging/release.py` reads them from here.

What goes here: only major new features and real changes to how something works, among them what your AI agents can do, and any change to safety, privacy or your data. One plain line each, saying what you can now do, not how the screen shows it. Never: changed wording, labels or layout, small fixes, or a feature's parts (its buttons, tabs, counts). Several changes to one feature are one line. A version with nothing major has one line, or none.

## 0.9.26, 12 November 2026

Each participant can have a picture, a label and a pen portrait, shown beside their name and, when you tick it, in your exports.
REFI-QDA files carry LPETs and LGETs too, out and back in.
Approving several of an agent's actions at once shows how far it has got, and Home says when actions wait for your yes.
AI boards, reviews and revisions on PETs, GETs and Themes are gone: an agent of your own through MCP does that work.
The demo's three interviews each have a recording, in more natural voices.

## 0.9.24, 10 November 2026

Idiograph runs on Linux: a .deb for Ubuntu and Debian, and an AppImage for other Linux.
Every PDF and picture is made by one typesetter, so a printout looks the same on a Mac, Windows and Linux.
Trajectories shows each trajectory as a band, with each participant's picture, label and tags, and each LPET's key quote and state at every timepoint.
Working together: a reviewer can suggest a new wording or title, and the owner accepts or declines it.
In a thematic analysis project, give an extract a code from the keyboard with quick keys.
Removing a project from Idiograph can move its folder to the Bin too.

## 0.9.23, 9 November 2026

Export a project, or the participants you choose, for NVivo, ATLAS.ti, MAXQDA and QualCoder, as a REFI-QDA file.
Real names in a transcript can be replaced as it comes in, from a list of names kept in the project's folder, and in transcripts already in.
AI features work with eight providers, among them Mistral in the EU, and Ollama or LM Studio on your own computer.
Working together: share a project with a colleague, each of you working on your own participants and reviewing the other's, through an end-to-end encrypted relay or a folder you both share.
Idiograph works better by keyboard and with a screen reader.

## 0.9.22, 7 November 2026

Search, yours and your AI agent's, can match whole words only, now the default, and match case.
Inside a project, the project's name opens its menu, the logo goes Home, and the statements screen has its own toolbar, docked or floating.
A shared copy opens in Idiograph with a double click on its .idiograph file.
When a shared copy comes back, you choose which boards to add, and every card keeps its place, even where you have since reworded or removed its statement.

## 0.9.21, 6 November 2026

Each project's own settings are in Project settings, opened from the project itself, and Settings holds the app's own.

## 0.9.20, 5 November 2026

The guidance and AI suggestions for statements and PET titles take a statement that says what the experience was like in the participant's own terms, and keep their ambivalence.
Your AI agent can read and set the statements' AI prompt.

## 0.9.19, 4 November 2026

Each project's history of changes, yours and your AI agent's, opens from Project history in its menu on Home.
Thematic analysis projects have Braun and Clarke's guidance beside Coding and the Themes board, and with AI features on, suggested codes and a review of your themes.
Home has Demo projects: the IPA demo and a new thematic analysis demo of the same interviews.
Settings, AI features keeps an agent log of each time Idiograph refused your AI agent in the last seven days.

## 0.9.18, 3 November 2026

A project can now follow reflexive thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke) as well as IPA: code the passages, keep one code list across participants, and build your themes on a board.
Only Idiograph's own window can now approve your AI agent's held changes or turn on Dangerously skip permissions, so no other program on your computer can.

## 0.9.17, 2 November 2026

When more than one change from your own AI agent waits, Review opens one card listing them all, each ticked, with Details for any one, so you approve or decline them together.
Going back from a passage opened on LGETs, or on a later timepoint's PETs, returns to that board where you were.
Ticks look the same in every window and at every size, in light and dark.

## 0.9.16, 1 November 2026

The table of PETs and the GETs print can Save as PDF, straight beside the workbook, with the same pages as Print.
Export follows the view: in Table view the table has Print, Save as PDF, Microsoft Word, Markdown and CSV with their ticks, and on the canvas Print, Save as PDF and Save as an image.
The printed table of PETs reads as a document: rows flow across pages, a heading never ends a page, the type is clearer, and the head is compact with the project and research question beside the participant. The GET table's head is the same.
Every printed page's foot names the participant and board, the project and the table, your name, Confidential and the date, and the page. Your name, in Settings, is the name your documents carry.
Dragging a PET or sub-PET, the group under the pointer stays still and is outlined, so its title is easy to reach. Let go on a title or in a body to put it inside.
A PET's or sub-PET's description shows whole on the canvas.
After your own AI agent changes a board, no card is left over another.
Quit on the Mac no longer risks a crash as the window closes.

## 0.9.15, 31 October 2026

Quotes on cards has three settings, Off, Short and Full, and Full shows every quote whole.
A double-click on a statement card edits it, on PETs, GETs, LGETs and LPETs, as a title renames, Go to the passage is in the card's menu, and each edit leaves a line in the board's History.
A quote can take in her passage just before or after, with only the interviewer between, and the card shows a time range.
Switching to another board opens on that board's own cards, not blank canvas.
A sub-PET can be dragged close under two side-by-side sub-PETs again.
With the window's text larger, the statements card stays in view beside a long passage, and Support, What's new and Settings no longer open scrolled or with extra scroll bars.
The statement count stays steady as you move between passages, and History lists every save at its own time.
For your own AI agents, Find and replace now reaches the LPETs and LGETs boards too.

## 0.9.14, 30 October 2026

Support Idiograph has Contribute code, and its costs in pounds, and it and Home's preview note ask you to keep the preview to yourself for now.

Settings, General shows the exact text size when it sits between Small, Medium and Large.
For your own AI agents, a statement written after its board was made can now be moved, stacked and put on the board, and loose cards they move back to Not placed now go there.

## 0.9.1, 20 October 2026

Idiograph opens in a window of its own, with its own menus, where it opened in your browser before. Your settings, projects and cases stay where they were.
Idiograph can update itself. It checks once a day, and in Settings you choose whether it downloads a new version without asking.
Search finds words anywhere in your work, a passage, a statement, a note, a theme, a record or a claim. Press Cmd + F, or Ctrl + F on Windows, on any screen.
The transcript, your statements and your exploratory notes stand side by side in three columns, in the order you choose, with experiential statements, PETs and GETs in a row at the top, and a small map in the corner shows where you are in a long transcript and on the PETs and GETs boards.
Quotes in place of highlights, a new statement from words you select, the guidance in a sheet you open when you want it, a participant's name you can change, and each participant in a colour of their own on the GETs board.
Sift and Trace say what went wrong in plain words, and every screen is easier to read in both themes.
Bring in a Word table, a CSV or an Excel file with a chooser on every column and a preview of what will land, see each statement's quote on its card with a button on the status bar, and arrange the GETs board with a row for each participant.
Back and Forward between the screens: from a passage back to the board you came from, and on again.
