# Guidance rail

The eleven lines the app shows in the guidance panel on the statements screen, distilled from
Adam's own guide to experiential statements and revised by Adam on 21 October 2026. Each line
starts with `- ` and the server reads them in that order, so edit them here and they change in the app
on the next load. The plain paragraph under the heading is shown under the list in the panel, so it can
be reworded here too. Keep both short enough to take in at a glance.

Under each line, indented by two spaces, sit its two examples, one line starting `- Less this: ` and one
starting `- More this: `, each a short statement on the demo's invented topic, sharing a home with a dog.
The panel opens them in two columns from the small arrow at the end of the line. The server reads an
indented line as the example of the line above it, never as a line of its own, and a line with no
examples shows no arrow.

## Holding the craft

1 and 2 are about the experience and interpretation, 3 and 4 about relevance. 5 to 8 are about form, more a
matter of judgement, so a statement that breaks one may show the line is wrong. 9 to 11 are about what a
statement carries: a movement, their stance kept whole, and force. 9 and 11 are project rules.

- Say what the experience was like for them, or what they made of it, with their stance. Not a bare event, a narrative fact or a topic label.
  - Less this: Walks the dog twice a day
  - More this: The daily walk as a welcome reason to go out
- Prefer your own interpretative words. Leave the participant's distinctive words to the quote.
  - Less this: "He's my little shadow"
  - More this: Being followed about the house as a comfort
- Lead with the phenomenon your research question asks about, not the person.
  - Less this: She feels guilty leaving him alone
  - More this: Guilt at leaving the dog alone
- Stay with the research question, not the interview or incidental facts.
  - Less this: Adopted a three-year-old spaniel in 2019
  - More this: Adoption as a promise to be kept
- One idea per statement. Beware commas and sub-clauses that treat two ideas, or two conflicting ones, as one.
  - Less this: Walks as freedom, though the rain makes them a chore
  - More this: Walks as freedom
- Use the words it needs and no more. Find the level of abstraction that is concise yet broad.
  - Less this: Feeling wanted when the dog waits at the door every evening after work
  - More this: Feeling wanted at the end of the day
- Make each statement stand alone. If it does not make sense without the transcript, revise it.
  - Less this: Relief when that stopped
  - More this: Relief when the night barking stopped
- Not every passage needs an experiential statement. Don't add one for the sake of it.
  - Less this: Directions to the park
  - More this: No statement, for a passage that gives directions to the park
- Say the movement in it. Show the turn in their experience, from one thing to another, a cost or a reversal, not a static label.
  - Less this: Feelings about the dog's illness
  - More this: From dreading the vet's news to being glad of every ordinary day
- Keep their stance whole. Where they are of two minds, don't smooth it into gladness. Give each side its own statement if it needs one.
  - Less this: Glad of the early walks
  - More this: Putting up with the early walks
- Give it force. Make it vivid and memorable in your words, and let it land on first reading.
  - Less this: Some mixed feelings about the dog's age
  - More this: Watching the dog slow down as watching time pass

## Placeholders

The craft prompts the statement field shows one of, a fresh one on every passage. One per line, the
app picks at random, so add, cut or reword them freely. Keep them to a question that can be held
in mind while reading the passage.

These lines start with `* ` rather than `- ` on purpose. The server reads each section by its
heading and takes either marker, so both work here. A server started before this section existed
swept the whole file for `- ` instead, and would have put all of these in the rail and in the
prompt until it was restarted.

* What is the core experiential aspect of this passage?
* What are they noticing, doing, feeling here?
* What is at stake for them in this?
* How do they take it?
* Their words, then yours: what do they call it?
* What changes for them between the start and the end of this passage?
* What are they making of it, as they say it?
* What was it like for them, in their own terms?
