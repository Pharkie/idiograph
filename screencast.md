# Idiograph screencast, the script

A quick screencast on the demo case for the IPA email group, before any release. About seven minutes. Spoken lines in plain type, what is on screen in italic. The demo is an invented interview with a participant called Juno about sharing a home with a dog, so nothing real reaches the screen.

Before recording: Reset demo from the landing page so Juno opens with the head start and no boards. A desk width, the light theme, AI suggestions switched on in Settings with a key set, so the three AI controls show. Close the shortcuts card. Work in rounds, under Settings, Experiential statements, stays off as on a new install. Have Bramble's name in mind, it comes up.

## 1. The three principles (40 seconds)

*The landing page, All cases, Recent, and the Demo section with Juno.*

This is Idiograph. It is a writing surface for the middle of an IPA analysis, from a transcript to experiential statements, and from the statements to personal experiential themes. It runs on your own computer and writes into the Excel workbook you already use. Before I show it, three principles, because every design decision in it answers to one of them.

Human first, AI assisted. You make every decision. The AI makes informed suggestions, a candidate statement, a proposed board, three titles, each one there to be weighed, taken, changed or ignored. It is the assistant, never the author.

Your files, your machine. Nothing in the cloud. It writes into your workbook and keeps plain files beside it that any tool can read. No account, no subscription. Participant data never leaves your machine unless you send a passage to an AI model, on your own key, pay as you go, or a local model and nothing leaves at all.

IPA by the book. Built on the method's philosophical ground, phenomenological, hermeneutic and idiographic, every default set by its key literature. Smith, Flowers and Larkin's steps, guidance, vocabulary and tables, experiential statements, PETs and GETs, and one case in depth before any case meets another. Personal habits are settings, never defaults.

## 2. Experiential statements (2 minutes)

*Open Juno. The statements screen: the sheet on the left, the fields in the middle, the guidance rail on the right. The research question in the band under the bar.*

This is Experiential statements. The transcript sits on the left as a sheet, one participant passage at a time, the passage in front of me large and the rest dimmed around it. The research question stays in view the whole time, because the statements answer it. The bar at the top says which passage I am on. Rounds of ten minutes are a setting, off unless I tick it.

*Click a timecode in the margin. The recording plays from there. Pause it.*

The recording is here, cued to the passage. A click on a timecode plays from that moment, so I am never working from the words alone.

*Go to the passage at 04:31, the walk. Read it. Select "I did not expect to hear myself say that" and press q.*

I read the passage, and I select the words that carry it and press q. That is a quote, verbatim, in inverted commas, on its own line. I can build one quote from two pieces of the same passage with Cmd held, and it joins them with an ellipsis in transcript order.

*Type an exploratory note in the notes field. Then a statement in the statement field, one line, such as "Loving a walk that was resented for a year". The word count under it.*

Exploratory notes here. Then the experiential statement, one per line, in the participant's register and not mine. The count under the field keeps me honest, and a line over fifteen words is flagged.

*Press the AI suggest button under the statement field. Three candidates come back. Take none, or take one and change it.*

Every AI control in the app carries this sparkles mark, and nothing else does, so you always know what is a model and what is you. AI suggest offers candidate statements for this passage, from the project's own guidance on what a statement is. They are candidates. I can take one, change it, or ignore all three, and the field is mine either way.

*Press Next, or Cmd + Enter. The next passage comes up, and the bar's count moves.*

Next writes the three fields into the workbook, the same EN and ES workbook I open in Excel, and goes on. Skip reads a passage and writes nothing. Nothing is written that I did not press Next on.

## 3. PETs (2 minutes 30)

*Press p. The PETs screen: eighteen cards in a round pile in the middle of the canvas, the counts top right, the bin bottom left.*

This is PETs, the step after the statements. Every statement in the case is a card, with its timecode. I group them on a canvas the way I did on Miro and before that on the floor. The wheel zooms, Space and a drag pans, a plain drag draws a lasso.

*Lasso the four cards about the morning stairs, press g, type "Cracking first, every morning", Enter. A label stands above them with a frame and a tint.*

Select the cards, press g, give the PET a title. The frame holds them, and from now on where a card sits inside the frame carries meaning: higher is the better example, and the draft follows that order.

*Drag the label. The PET moves as one, leaning as it goes. Drag one card out a long way. The frame stretches to follow it.*

A PET moves as one body. A card dragged away does not leave its PET, the frame stretches to keep it, because leaving is a decision and not an accident. Alt held on the drop takes it out, and a tip says so while I drag.

*Click the label: nothing opens. Press i: the sidebar opens on the Inspector. It shows the PET, every quote under it with a play button and a tick. Play one. Tick one.*

A click only selects. The Inspector follows the selection and lists every quote under the PET, row by row. Each one plays at its timecode. A tick picks the one or two quotes that carry the PET, and those go into the draft. Under the title is a box for a note on the PET, my own words on why it exists, which also goes into the draft.

*Drag a card to the bin. Click the bin. The list, with Put back.*

A statement I reject on second thoughts goes to the bin. The bin is never emptied. Nothing on this screen deletes a statement, and nothing on this screen writes the workbook.

*Cmd + Z twice. The card comes back out of the bin, the pick is unticked.*

Undo runs through all of it.

*Board menu, Duplicate, rename the copy "Mornings apart". Board menu, Mark as current: a ribbon beside the name.*

A grouping I want to try beside another is a board. Duplicate keeps this one and gives me a copy to argue with, and the one I am going with I mark as Current. Each board keeps its own history, a revision every fifteen minutes while I work and one whenever I ask, and any revision can be viewed, restored, or copied from.

## 4. The AI as assistant (1 minute 30)

*Board menu, New AI board, up to 5 PETs, Make. Wait. "Board 2" comes on the canvas, "with AI" after its name in the board menu: four or five PETs in round blobs, the leftovers in Not placed to the right, each with its reason.*

New AI board, in the board menu, makes a board of its own and leaves mine untouched. The prompt carries the IPA guidance file that ships with the app, Smith, Flowers and Larkin and Smith and Nizza in their own words: a PET holds more than one statement, leave out only what does not answer the research question, up to five PETs unless it gives a reason. What it leaves out stays in Not placed for me, never in the bin. I can read its board, copy a PET across to mine with a right-click, or delete it from the menu. Nothing it did moved a card of mine.

*Switch back to Mornings apart. The sidebar's Review tab, AI review. The questions come back, each a card with the rule it rests on and a link to the PET.*

AI review asks questions of my board from the same guidance, in a reviewer's hedged first person. My sense is, I could not see, I was left wondering. Never a verdict and never a new grouping. I mark one Considered, dismiss another, and the review stays with the board.

*Select a label, press n. Three titles come back in the Inspector. Edit one.*

And three titles for a PET, each from a different angle, to take, edit or ignore.

## 5. What leaves the app (40 seconds)

*Export, .md and CSV. The line names the two files. Show in Finder. Open the markdown draft beside the workbook.*

Export writes a PET draft in markdown, PET by PET in canvas order with the picked quotes, and the statements CSV with a Proposed PET column, both into Working beside the workbook.

*Export, Table. The print view, the table of PETs on landscape A4, each statement beside its quote. Save as PDF.*

And a printable view, landscape A4, which saves as a PDF from the print dialog.

*The Finder window: the workbook, Working with the progress file, the themes file and the draft.*

Everything is here. The workbook Excel opens, a progress file, and the themes file with every board and its history, plain JSON any tool can read. No project format, no database, no account.
